Memory benchmarks are easy to misread. This page separates what MemOS measures
from what it does not, and gives the exact numbers with their caveats.

## Two tracks

| Track | What it measures | Judge |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Retrieval-only | Did the right evidence come back? | None; evidence IDs |
| End-to-end | Did the model answer correctly? | An LLM judge |

MemOS publishes retrieval-only numbers by default. They are deterministic and
reproducible. End-to-end numbers depend on the reader model, the judge model,
and the judge prompt, so they are reported separately and never merged into the
retrieval table.

## Retrieval results

| Benchmark | Score | Conditions |
| --- | --- | --- |
| LongMemEval-S, full 500 | **99.4% Hit@20** · 97.8% all-evidence@20 | one node per session, `nomic-embed-text`, contextual header, no reranker |
| ConvoMem, 387 scored | **91.0% Hit@10** · 89.4% evidence recall@10 | message evidence, `all-minilm`, contextual header |
| LoCoMo, convs 0–1 | **79.8% Hit@10** · 75.9% evidence recall@10 | 304 questions, same embedder, previous-turn context |
| LoCoMo, 1,986 questions | **64.1% Hit@10** | full set, retrieval-only |
| HotPotQA distractor | **100% Hit@10** · 96.3% evidence recall@10 | 500 validation questions |

Every number is scored by evidence-ID matching. No LLM judge is in the loop.

End-to-end is reported separately: **78%** on a 50-question LongMemEval-S
sample, self-judged with the same model. That is not comparable to vendor
numbers produced with a gpt-4o judge.

## Why the numbers are not directly comparable

- **Recall is not accuracy.** A system can retrieve the right evidence and
  still answer wrong. MemOS reports both retrieval and, separately, a small
  self-judged end-to-end sample.
- **LoCoMo has a ceiling.** An independent audit found 6.4% of the answer key
  is wrong, so a perfect system caps out near 93.6%, not 100%.
- **The judge is lenient.** The standard LoCoMo judge accepts a large share of
  intentionally wrong but topically adjacent answers. Judge-prompt changes
  alone have moved scores by 40 points.
- **`top_k` can bypass retrieval.** If `top_k` is larger than the number of
  sessions, the whole corpus is in the candidate pool. MemOS reports the
  session granularity and the exact command.

<Callout type="note" title="What we do not claim">
MemOS does not claim a state-of-the-art end-to-end QA score. The retrieval
numbers are strong and reproducible; the end-to-end measurement is a separate,
smaller sample. Treat vendor numbers that do not disclose the harness as
marketing.
</Callout>

## Reproduce it

The harness lives in the repository:

```bash
# Retrieval-only, no API key
pnpm bench:longmemeval:retrieve
pnpm bench:locomo:retrieve
pnpm bench:convomem

# End-to-end, needs a reader and judge
pnpm bench:longmemeval
pnpm bench:locomo
```

Artifacts and per-category breakdowns are committed under `docs/artifacts/`.
The commands, caveats, and retractions are in
[`benchmarks/README.md`](https://github.com/Markgatcha/memos/blob/main/benchmarks/README.md).

## The open leaderboard

The [leaderboard spec](/docs/memos/leaderboard) defines a submission format
that requires:

- the harness and dataset version,
- the judge model and prompt hash for end-to-end runs,
- at least three runs with standard deviation,
- per-question artifacts,
- and a reproduction command.

The goal is not to declare a winner. It is to make every published number
checkable.
